# Live verification of pet-travel requirements

## Scope and conclusions

Zurtex needs an evidence-checking system that can distinguish a current regulation, a bilateral export certificate, an operational authority instruction and an unverified summary. Checking that a government URL responds does not establish that a requirement remains correct. The same applies to finding matching words in a search snippet. A credible result must identify what was read, what specific claim it supports, when it was checked, which animal and journey conditions it covers, and which questions remain unresolved.

This assessment uses official sources checked on **12 September 2026**. Its sample covers Great Britain–Brazil, Hong Kong–Japan, mainland United States–Australia and Great Britain–France, with separate United States return and transit cases. The sample intentionally contains different regulatory structures. It is not an audit of every selectable country, airline or possible animal history. “Great Britain” means England, Scotland and Wales; Northern Ireland requires separate treatment.

The strongest live-research finding is a conflict in the Great Britain–Brazil source chain. MAPA says its newer certificate model is mandatory, while the active APHA listing links an older specimen. The correct response is to show the mismatch and ask the responsible authorities to reconcile it. An open-weight model should not choose one certificate on the customer's behalf or silently combine incompatible time limits. [^1][^2][^3][^4]

The strongest architectural finding is that the route is not the regulatory unit. Species, recent residence, vaccination history, owner travel, transfer of ownership, region, exact port and connections determine which route rules apply. A return journey must inherit the places visited on the outward journey. Merely reversing two country codes can select the wrong United States or Great Britain requirements.

The supplied PetsCleared visual reference is a design reference, not evidence of its implementation, model choice, source-verification method or legal accuracy. Nothing in these observations establishes how that service works internally.

## Sample 1: Great Britain to Brazil

### Source chain and unresolved document conflict

MAPA's entry page directs travellers to the CVI model implementing Portaria 741/2024 and says it became mandatory on 6 September 2025. It also identifies an official passport alternative and limited reuse of certain MAPA-issued outward CVIs for return to Brazil. The passport alternative must satisfy Brazil's requirements; an ordinary vaccination record does not establish this. [^1]

APHA's EHC 2906 page is marked active and was updated on 6 May 2026. Great Britain applicants use the online EHC process. Its linked version-4 specimen is marked as agreed in 2014 and includes a 60-day validity statement plus an ambiguous 10-day border note. This creates an unresolved compatibility question with MAPA's current model, rather than proof that either complete process has been withdrawn. [^2][^3]

The EHC guidance, revised 26 October 2023, expressly tells exporters to resolve mismatches between the certificate and the importing authority's conditions with APHA/DAERA. It also warns against manual alterations to final certificates. This supports treating the discrepancy as a real action item, not hiding it beneath “sources verified.” [^4]

### Verified baseline, with distinct timing anchors

Portaria 741 provides the baseline: valid rabies vaccination from 90 days of age, a 21-day primary-vaccination interval before shipment, parasite treatment within 15 days before CVI issue, and clinical examination within 10 days before CVI issue. A lapsed booster becomes primary vaccination. The CVI has a 60-calendar-day period subject to valid rabies coverage. Younger-animal and recognised rabies-free-origin exceptions require specific certification. Where a microchip is used, its standard and location are specified; that clause does not itself establish a universal microchip mandate. Prior leishmaniasis, identification and additional restrictions deserve individual review. [^5]

These are separate clocks: **vaccination → shipment**, **treatment → certificate issue**, **examination → certificate issue**, and **certificate issue → document validity**. They must not all become “days before arrival.” Because the bilateral certificate discrepancy remains open, the product should present preparation intervals without automatically promising the final issue or entry window.

### Return to Great Britain and departure from Brazil

Brazil is absent from the current Great Britain listed-country table. The unlisted-country pathway adds rabies testing and the appropriate Great Britain travel document. The blood-test page specifies sampling at least 30 days after vaccination, an approved laboratory, a result of at least 0.5 IU/ml and a three-month wait, with a documented return exception. That exception must be checked against the actual pre-departure records; “originally from Britain” is insufficient. [^6][^7]

A dog returning from Brazil also needs veterinary tapeworm treatment in the 24–120-hour period before Great Britain entry. Product, date, time and veterinary authentication matter, and a delayed flight can invalidate the window. This is a dog requirement and must not be applied to cats. [^8]

MAPA's exit page currently places Great Britain e-CVI applications in a 9-to-3-day pre-shipment window, requires the application through an authorised veterinarian, and specifies physical endorsement. That application period does not replace Great Britain's entry requirements. Other destinations have different digital/paper workflows; export handling must therefore be destination-specific. [^9]

**Open points:** exact acceptance of the Great Britain model, any supplementary bilateral conditions, actual border handling, local/breed rules, transit and the pet's records. No blanket no-quarantine, no-permit or unrestricted-breed conclusion is justified from this sample.

## Sample 2: Hong Kong to Japan

### Japan entry requirements

Hong Kong is not among AQS's designated regions. Those regions are Australia, New Zealand, Iceland, Fiji, Hawaii and Guam, and even their lighter pathway depends on qualifying residence and transit history. Country-of-departure labels alone cannot select the exemption. [^10]

For the ordinary non-designated pathway, AQS requires identification before qualifying rabies vaccination, a first dose at age 91 days or older, and a second dose at least 30 days later while the first remains valid. Blood sampling follows the second dose; same-day sampling is possible. A designated laboratory must return at least 0.5 IU/ml. AQS rejects rabies RNA vaccines for this pathway. Its pre-export clinical examination is within 10 days before boarding and checks dogs for leptospirosis as well as rabies. [^11]

The detailed AQS guide anchors the 180-day wait to **blood sampling**, counting sampling as day zero. Arrival must remain within the rabies vaccination and test-validity periods. Advance notification goes to the intended entry office at least 40 days before arrival. Incomplete preparation can lead to detention or return; the guide explains conditional retesting exceptions, so a later titre must not automatically restart every animal's waiting period. [^12]

AQS states that compliant import quarantine can finish within 12 hours; this is conditional on inspection and documentation. Its main page also contains laboratory-revocation notices. Approval of the testing laboratory is therefore live information, not a timeless property copied into a static country guide. [^13]

### Hong Kong export and Japanese return departure

AFCD distinguishes issuing a government certificate, certifying information and endorsing a private veterinarian's certificate. Its service page calls for destination requirements, supporting records, and the appropriate certification service. An official government examination requires an appointment at least ten working days ahead. This booking lead time is distinct from Japan's short final clinical-examination window. [^14]

For a return or onward departure from Japan, AQS's export page requires an inspection application at least 14 days before the desired inspection date. The final inspection normally falls within ten days before departure unless the destination specifies otherwise. Reusing an old “seven days” summary would miss the current instruction. [^15]

**Open points:** actual vaccination records, recognised laboratory status, previous Japanese export/return documentation, exact entry airport and Hong Kong re-entry permit conditions. The researched Japan entry process does not independently establish Hong Kong return eligibility.

## Sample 3: mainland United States to Australia

### Country group, preparation and quarantine

DAFF places the United States **excluding Hawaii and Guam** in Group 3. Its guide requires approved-country preparation and a 180-day period from **laboratory receipt of the RNATT sample**, not sample collection. RNATT validity is 12 months from sampling. Identity verification before the RNATT can reduce minimum post-entry quarantine from 30 to 10 days; it is not a general promise of a ten-day release. The issued import permit and booked quarantine remain necessary. [^16]

This route illustrates why a shared “rabies wait: 180 days” value is inadequate. Japan measures from sampling; Australia measures from laboratory receipt. A source model must retain both the event and unit, and date calculations must take the actual event record. Approved-origin residency also cannot be obtained by booking a short connection through an approved airport.

### United States operational evidence

APHIS's Australia page describes the United States identity-declaration workflow, including two accredited-veterinarian declarations and USDA's separate endorsed identity declaration. The traveller keeps the endorsed declaration with the export documents. An ordinary chip certificate is not equivalent evidence. APHIS also specifies permitted test methods and the rabies declaration process. [^17]

The dog pathway includes disease-specific testing and parasite preparation; exact methods and products remain attached to the current permit and complete species guide. Dog and cat pathways must remain separate. [^16][^17]

### Transport and live limitations

The ordinary Group 3 guide requires manifested cargo arriving directly at Melbourne International Airport for quarantine. It distinguishes staying on the aircraft from changing aircraft. A rule engine must capture this difference before determining whether a connection preserves eligibility. [^16]

**Open points:** final permit, whether the identity check qualifies for reduced quarantine, detailed residence records, health/test records, source region within the United States, flight handling and any assistance-dog exception. Carrier acceptance and the import permit are separate decisions.

## Sample 4: Great Britain to France

The European Commission's current guidance uses the 2026 framework and distinguishes entry from onward travel. An AHC is normally valid ten days for the first entry check, with a sea-journey extension, and up to six months for subsequent EU movement, or rabies expiry if earlier. Older-model AHCs remain acceptable if issued before 1 October 2026. [^18]

Great Britain's own AHC guidance states that issue day counts as day one for the ten-day entry period. It also requires waiting for primary vaccination to become effective before AHC issue, with at least 21 full days and possible longer product-specific intervals. Thus “arrival minus 21 days” is not a certified vaccination deadline. AHC issue date, vaccine valid-from date and the first EU border all matter. [^19][^20]

The Commission's unlisted-origin rule now anchors the 90-day rabies-test interval to **AHC issue**, not simply to the flight. That requirement should not be applied to a qualifying listed-origin case merely because a result template contains a titre section. Owner accompaniment, the five-day authorised-person exception, group size and sale/transfer purpose are also explicit pathway conditions. [^18]

France's June 2026 official note adds national detail: spontaneous presentation to customs, dangerous-dog categories, certain hybrid-cat documentation and a permanent-tooth condition for dogs imported from third countries. These details are easy to miss when reading only generic EU guidance. A breed name alone may not establish the legal category, so a concrete breed review remains necessary. [^21]

Tapeworm treatment is not a blanket France-entry step. However, the return dog journey to Great Britain requires its own treatment window and accepted travel document. The AHC's return validity should not be confused with permission to use it for repeated new departures from Great Britain. [^8][^19]

**Open points:** puppy eligibility, veterinary vaccine validity, owner residence/passport status, breed/type, actual first EU entry, whether the movement remains non-commercial and return date. Some authority text differs in how it explains elapsed days; calculate from the issued veterinary record, not an inferred universal vaccine interval.

## United States re-entry and transit

### Three distinct dog pathways

For a dog that has spent the entire preceding six months only in CDC dog-rabies-free or low-risk places, CDC requires its Dog Import Form receipt, apparent health, age of at least six months and a universally readable microchip. The low-risk receipt can cover repeated trips for six months under its conditions, but a different departure country requires a new receipt. These are CDC conditions, not a substitute for state or agriculture requirements. [^22]

A United States-vaccinated dog with high-risk history needs the specific Certification of U.S.-issued Rabies Vaccination, completed and USDA-endorsed before leaving the United States, plus the import-form receipt. A normal rabies certificate is insufficient. Export certificates issued after 31 July 2025 no longer substitute; a qualifying older certificate has its own conditions. Without the appropriate pre-departure document, CDC directs the dog to the foreign-vaccinated pathway. [^23]

The foreign-vaccinated high-risk pathway requires government-endorsed vaccination/microchip certification, the import receipt and a reservation at a CDC-registered animal care facility. A valid titre avoids the ordinary additional 28-day quarantine, but not the examination/revaccination requirement. The initial-dose and sample timing rules differ; continuous valid booster history changes when sampling can occur. Arrival must be at the airport associated with the reserved facility, with onward domestic travel after release. [^24]

### Connections and history

CDC's layover guidance explicitly distinguishes cargo or excess baggage retained by the airline, no customs clearance, and onward travel to another country. Its exemption requires all four conditions. Clearing customs in a high-risk country during a connection changes the later United States pathway; cabin travel and airline changes can affect that fact. [^25]

A country-risk registry should be drawn from CDC's **dog importation** list. Human-traveller rabies advice is a different dataset. Brazil appears on the high-risk dog-importation list, so a Brazil return cannot inherit a low-risk pathway simply because the final connecting airport is elsewhere. [^26]

The cat path must be separately sourced and kept out of CDC dog-form logic. This study does not finish all United States local requirements or a full cat-import review. State/territory selection and APHIS animal-disease controls remain separate layers.

## Implementation requirements

### Evidence record

Each extracted claim needs: claim ID, route/species scope, factual statement, source URL and title, authority, retrieval timestamp, source publication/effective dates where available, source passage locator, short supporting excerpt, conditions and exceptions, relevant event/date anchor, baseline version, and a verification result. The result set should include `supported`, `changed`, `conflicting`, `not_found`, `source_unavailable` and `needs_case_details`. A fetched page and a supported claim are different events.

The model must receive the baseline claim and fetched source content as separate data. Retrieved text, PDFs and search snippets are untrusted input. Embedded instructions may not alter source policy, request secrets, change application state or instruct the model to mark a claim verified. Model output must pass structural validation, cite actually fetched evidence, preserve source identity, and remain bounded to the research task.

Source excerpts alone do not prove every qualifier. The system should check the authority, jurisdiction, species, purpose and effective period before matching a claim. Missing evidence becomes unresolved. Conflicting authority text becomes a visible conflict. Negative claims such as “no permit,” “no quarantine,” or “no breed restrictions” require direct evidence covering the exact conditions; absence of a paragraph is insufficient.

### Progress and caching

The loading screen should reflect completed backend stages: request scoped, export sources read, entry sources read, records/timing compared, and guide assembled. Network timeouts should finish with a limited result and source failure, not a success animation. A percentage can represent completed work units; it should not pretend to estimate unknown model time or confidence.

Cache keys must include direction, species, relevant region, journey purpose, owner timing, transit/handling details, risk-history classification, source set and baseline version. Cached reports must show their actual check time. A fresh request can reuse a permitted fresh cache without claiming a new live read. Changes to the country list, model version, extraction rules or source content should invalidate dependent reports. The product should support reopening sources and retrying unavailable evidence.

### Result presentation

The result should begin with the route, animal and travel dates, followed by immediate actions and open blockers. A useful checklist groups tasks by the work required: eligibility and records, identification/rabies, permits, veterinary appointments, documents, transport and arrival. Each task should show who performs it, its timing anchor, relevant sources and whether it depends on missing facts. Outbound and return must be distinct assessments; unknown dates should say “date not set” without fabricated calendar deadlines.

A global green “verified” badge is inappropriate when only some claims have evidence. Prefer a clear statement such as “Six requirements matched current official sources; two need confirmation.” Live model verification is still a comparison of public information; it is not an authority's acceptance of a pet's documents.

## Static-library review and integration hazards

The existing dossiers already preserve important distinctions: Japanese residence eligibility, Australian laboratory-receipt timing, 2026 EU certificate changes, CDC vaccination/history pathways and the limits of departure-country assumptions. These should survive the result redesign.

The initial library contained no Brazil dossier. This work adds a separate dated dossier with seven import and three export tasks, source-backed caveats and the Great Britain document-conflict task. The country registry, research links and dossier index now include Brazil. The addition deliberately does not declare the source mismatch resolved.

Adding a country exposes a separate risk in finite origin-code lists. At the time of this review, calling the draft composer for Brazil origins omitted France and Great Britain's unlisted-origin titre tasks. It also produced normal Australia and New Zealand preparation without an unsupported-origin blocker. These are integration findings, not proof that Brazil has an approved direct pathway. Origin eligibility must default to unknown until matched against a destination's current complete classification, rather than default to allowed because an explicit denylist lacks a newly added country.

The implementation now includes Brazil in the researched EU and Great Britain unlisted-origin branches and Japan's non-designated branch. The EU classification was checked against the Commission's current complete exemption list, in which Brazil is absent. Australia direct-import preparation is withheld for Brazil. New Zealand preparation is also withheld because no approved Brazil-origin pathway has been established: live attempts to open MPI's current approval schedule and 2026 guide returned HTTP 403, so this addition does not claim a freshly verified New Zealand classification. Other destinations with a missing origin-group mapping receive an explicit classification gap instead of a misleading partial veterinary sequence. [^27][^28]

A separate factual error was found and corrected: the United States baseline classified the United Arab Emirates as low-risk despite its presence in CDC's April 2026 high-risk list. Both UAE and Brazil are now assigned to the high-risk departure group, and the low-risk-only step is withheld for known high-risk origins. Low-risk departures retain history-dependent alternatives because the previous six months remain unknown. [^26]

The existing composer also calculates a generic EU primary-vaccine planning date by subtracting 21 days from arrival. The notice that product requirements can be longer is valuable, but the date should remain a tentative planning marker or be withheld until vaccination valid-from and AHC issue dates are known. Final treatment windows measured in hours require time zone and arrival time; a date-only field cannot validate them.

US history-dependent alternatives are intentionally conditional in the baseline. A live source reader must not promote one to the selected pathway when the request does not contain six months of animal history. Likewise, completion of research does not remove user-specific unknowns.

## Critique and revision

The first possible design—one current-source check per country—would have missed the Brazil certificate conflict, Australian identity workflow and return-history changes. The revised direction makes a requirement, its source and its applicability the unit of verification. It also separates regulatory facts from whether the particular animal meets them.

The remaining weakness is breadth. This sample cannot substantiate a claim that every offered country, every carrier and every transit is deeply verified. The product should expose the actual scope of each run and retain its gaps. A small model can help compare bounded source passages to baseline claims; it must not supply missing evidence from its weights. Source failures, country additions and classifier edge cases deserve the same attention as happy-path presentation.

The focused regression checks cover Brazil's EU/GB rabies branches, Australia/New Zealand withholding, the GB–Brazil certificate conflict, CDC Brazil/UAE classification, dog/cat separation, unclassified origins and source integrity in both directions. The matching documentation and all 552 country-pair compositions also passed their current checks. These tests verify the implemented boundaries and references; they do not turn every composed route into a complete regulatory audit.
