# Canada: dog and cat travel research

Research snapshot: 2026-09-11. Status: researched with gaps. Publication: draft. Human approval: none.

[Research index](../README.md) · [Scope, findings and conflict register](../2026-09-11-country-requirements.md)

## Scope and conclusion

DRAFT pending owner review. Personal dogs and domestic cats are assessed by age, purpose and origin. Unaccompanied young dogs have extra certification; high-risk commercial dog imports face a separate ban.

This dossier records both entry requirements and departure procedures. Its origin groups describe potential pathways, not a finding that an individual pet meets residence, vaccination, ownership or permit conditions. It is not an exhaustive commercial-import, assistance-animal, wildlife or local-keeping-law opinion.

## Origin-dependent pathways

### CFIA-designated rabies-free choices in the selected list

Catalogue jurisdictions: AU, GB, IE, JP, NZ.

May support the prescribed country-status certificate instead of rabies vaccination evidence where that age/purpose pathway permits it; residence and certificate wording still matter. [^2][^3]

### Other selected origins

Catalogue jurisdictions: US, FR, DE, NL, ES, IT, PT, CH, NO, AE, SG, HK, CN, TW, TH, MY, PH.

Use the non-rabies-free personal-pet branch and verify the separate commercial high-risk ban if purpose changes. [^2][^5]

## Entry: preparation and arrival actions

The order below is the editorial baseline. Select the appropriate branch and arrange early permit, quarantine and appointment tasks alongside veterinary preparation; do not wait until an earlier procedure is finished to start booking a later appointment. The application filters species/countries and stops unapproved direct AU/NZ pathways.

### 1. Choose the correct CFIA import scenario

Planning advice · Before booking

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- Record species, exact age on arrival, origin, ownership purpose and whether the owner accompanies the dog.
- Use CFIA's pet selector and AIRS for the actual journey, including temporary visits, transit and returning Canadian pets.

Evidence: [^2][^1]

### 2. Resolve any ownership-transfer or commercial purpose

Conditional · Before acquiring or booking the animal

Species: dog only.

- Adoption, fostering, resale, breeding, shows and research can fall within CFIA's commercial dog definition.
- Commercial dogs from designated high-risk dog-rabies countries are prohibited regardless of age. An unaccompanied personal pet is not automatically commercial.

Evidence: [^5][^2]

### 3. Document young-animal age

Conditional · At entry

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- For dogs and domestic cats younger than three months, obtain veterinary proof of age; the ordinary rabies-certification requirement does not apply.
- Do not infer transport fitness or an unaccompanied-puppy exemption from the rabies age exemption.

Evidence: [^3][^4]

### 4. Arrange the applicable rabies evidence

Conditional · Before departure; valid at entry

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- From three months of age, prepare a current veterinarian-signed rabies certificate; CFIA-designated rabies-free origins can offer a qualifying veterinary country-status certificate instead.
- Check the origin-status certificate's residence conditions, including six months or since birth for dogs. Do not substitute another country's rabies-risk list.

Evidence: [^3][^4]

### 5. Arrange the extra certificate for an unaccompanied young dog

Conditional · Veterinary examination no more than 72 hours before Canadian import

Species: dog only.

- For a personal dog under eight months travelling without its owner, obtain the additional veterinary certificate.
- It must document age of at least eight weeks at examination, clinical health, transport fitness and the required distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus and parainfluenza vaccination details.

Evidence: [^3]

### 6. Check certificate detail and any special permit case

Planning advice · Before final veterinary appointment

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- Use legible English or French documents, or the accepted official translation; include identifying details, vaccine name/serial number, administration date and validity.
- If vaccination is medically contraindicated or the animal has recent wild-hybrid ancestry, request CFIA's case-specific decision before travel. Do not assume that a titre or ordinary pet certificate grants an exemption.

Evidence: [^4]

### 7. Carry the records and complete arrival checks

Required within the applicable pathway · On arrival; coordinate special inspections beforehand

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- Present the pet and required original documents when requested for border inspection.
- Check the carrier's transport rules and the destination province or municipality for additional vaccination and breed conditions before travel.

Evidence: [^2][^4]

## Departure: exporting from Canada

These actions run alongside the destination's entry preparation. A destination certificate, import permit, airline reservation and export endorsement are different documents or decisions.

### 1. Obtain the destination and transit certificate requirements

Planning advice · Well before travel

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- Find the applicable negotiated CFIA export certificate and compare it with current destination requirements.
- If no prescribed certificate exists, ask whether the Canadian International Health Certificate is accepted; a new negotiated certificate can take months.

Evidence: [^6]

### 2. Book and complete any required CFIA endorsement

Conditional · Before leaving Canada; within destination timing

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- Arrange required tests and treatments with the private veterinarian and book the official CFIA endorsement appointment early.
- CFIA cannot endorse the export certificate after the animal has left Canada. Carry the completed destination documents and any import permit.

Evidence: [^6]

## Caveats

- DRAFT pending owner review; the adult personal-pet pathway cannot be used to approve a commercial or young-unaccompanied case.
- Air cargo does not by itself determine CFIA commercial status.
- Domestic cats and dogs have different age and document branches; hybrid animals require separate review.

## Conflicts and remaining verification gaps

- AIRS was not executed for every bilateral origin, age, purpose and transport scenario.
- The Import Reference Document mentions titre alternatives, while current pet-tool guidance sets out specific medical-exemption conditions; a titre-only case remains subject to CFIA confirmation.
- Provincial/municipal breed rules, exact inspection fees and each carrier's conditions have not been assessed.

These gaps are not waived by the research date. The relevant authority, certificate or issued permit must resolve them before owner approval or individual travel advice.
