# United States: dog and cat travel research

Research snapshot: 2026-09-11. Status: researched with gaps. Publication: draft. Human approval: none.

[Research index](../README.md) · [Scope, findings and conflict register](../2026-09-11-country-requirements.md)

## Scope and conclusion

DRAFT pending owner review. Federal dog entry depends on the previous six months of travel and where rabies vaccination was given. Cats follow a separate federal pathway; state and territory requirements remain additional.

This dossier records both entry requirements and departure procedures. Its origin groups describe potential pathways, not a finding that an individual pet meets residence, vaccination, ownership or permit conditions. It is not an exhaustive commercial-import, assistance-animal, wildlife or local-keeping-law opinion.

## Origin-dependent pathways

### CDC high-risk origins among the selected countries

Catalogue jurisdictions: CN, TH, MY, PH, BR, AE.

Any presence in one of these places within the preceding six months invokes the high-risk dog branch. Mainland China excludes Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan. [^2]

### CDC low-risk or rabies-free selected origins

Catalogue jurisdictions: CA, GB, IE, FR, DE, NL, ES, IT, PT, CH, NO, SG, HK, JP, AU, NZ, TW.

The simpler dog branch applies only if the entire preceding six months were spent in low-risk/rabies-free places. Spain's Ceuta and Melilla are separately listed high-risk places. [^2][^3]

## Entry: preparation and arrival actions

The order below is the editorial baseline. Select the appropriate branch and arrange early permit, quarantine and appointment tasks alongside veterinary preparation; do not wait until an earlier procedure is finished to start booking a later appointment. The application filters species/countries and stops unapproved direct AU/NZ pathways.

### 1. Identify the state or territory and the dog's travel history

Planning advice · Before booking

Species: dog only.

- Record the final state or territory, species, age, purpose and every country visited in the preceding six months.
- For dogs, any CDC high-risk-country stay changes the pathway even when the last departure airport is in a low-risk country.

Evidence: [^1][^2]

### 2. Check the minimum federal dog conditions

Required within the applicable pathway · Before booking; satisfied on arrival

Species: dog only.

- Every dog must be at least six months old, appear healthy and have a microchip detectable by a universal scanner.
- A dog with high-risk history must have a valid rabies vaccination; an unvaccinated dog with that history cannot enter.

Evidence: [^3][^1]

### 3. Complete the low-risk CDC form pathway if eligible

Conditional · Before travel; receipt normally valid for six months

Species: dog only.

- If the dog has been only in CDC low-risk or rabies-free places throughout the preceding six months, complete its own free CDC Dog Import Form.
- Carry the receipt and ensure its departure country is correct; a change of departure country or high-risk travel can require a new form. This is only the CDC document requirement.

Evidence: [^3]

### 4. Use the U.S.-vaccinated high-risk return pathway if documented

Conditional · Certification completed and USDA-endorsed before leaving the U.S.

Species: dog only.

- Carry the CDC Dog Import Form receipt and the specific Certification of U.S.-issued Rabies Vaccination completed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian and endorsed by USDA.
- A routine rabies certificate is insufficient. Export certificates issued after 31 July 2025 cannot substitute; without qualifying pre-departure certification, CDC directs the dog to the foreign-vaccinated pathway.

Evidence: [^4]

### 5. Prepare foreign-vaccinated dogs with high-risk history

Conditional · Begin at least 60 days ahead; foreign certification within 30 days of travel

Species: dog only.

- Microchip before rabies vaccination; the first accepted dose is at age 12 weeks or older and at least 28 days before entry.
- Arrange government endorsement of the Certification of Foreign Rabies Vaccination and Microchip and carry its printed version.
- For a qualifying titre, sample at least 30 days after the first valid dose and at least 28 days before entry; use a CDC-approved laboratory. Continuous booster histories have a specified sampling exception.
- Reserve a CDC-registered facility for examination and revaccination, plus 28-day quarantine if no valid titre; fly into its associated airport and await release before onward travel.

Evidence: [^5]

### 6. Check APHIS animal-disease conditions

Conditional · Before departure; screwworm examination within five days before shipment

Species: dog only.

- For dogs from screwworm-affected areas, obtain the prescribed government-veterinarian certificate confirming recent examination and freedom from infestation or completed treatment.
- For FMD-affected origins, remove excessive dirt, hay and straw from fur and bedding; bathe at destination and separate from livestock for five days. Check current affected-area lists.

Evidence: [^6]

### 7. Use the separate cat pathway

Required within the applicable pathway · Before departure and on arrival

Species: cat only.

- Cats must appear healthy and are subject to port inspection; illness can trigger a veterinary examination at the owner's cost.
- CDC does not require a cat rabies certificate or general pet health certificate. State, territory and carrier requirements can still require vaccination or veterinary paperwork.

Evidence: [^7]

### 8. Arrange Hawaii’s island-specific entry and quarantine process

Conditional · Before booking; Hawaii/Guam preparation can take months

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below. US arrival-region filter: hawaii.

- For Hawaii, select the actual island checklist; direct release is conditional on complete preparation, testing and paperwork. The current authority page also requires a New World Screwworm-freedom statement on required CVIs.
- Use the actual island’s current checklist and confirm document, testing and arrival-inspection requirements before booking. This preview does not assess eligibility for airport release.

Evidence: [^8][^9]

### 9. Obtain Guam’s entry permit and quarantine arrangements

Conditional · Before booking; Hawaii/Guam preparation can take months

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below. US arrival-region filter: guam.

- For Guam, obtain an entry permit and the applicable origin-specific quarantine arrangements; submit the application package at least 30 days ahead and schedule the final certificate within its short validity window.
- Obtain the authority’s origin-specific conditions and actual permit before fixing the flight. This preview does not establish a quarantine exemption.

Evidence: [^10]

### 10. Check the destination state’s arrival requirements

Conditional · Before booking; Hawaii/Guam preparation can take months

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below. US arrival-region filter: mainland, other.

- For other destinations, verify state/local vaccination, health-certificate and breed rules and carry all documents for inspection. Airline acceptance is a separate check.
- Check the actual state or territory’s authority, not only federal entry rules. State-specific certification is not yet assessed here.

Evidence: [^7][^11]

## Departure: exporting from United States

These actions run alongside the destination's entry preparation. A destination certificate, import permit, airline reservation and export endorsement are different documents or decisions.

### 1. Build the destination-specific veterinary plan

Planning advice · As soon as travel is considered

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- Contact a USDA-accredited veterinarian and obtain current destination and transit requirements.
- Schedule identification, vaccines, tests, treatments and any destination import permit in the required order.

Evidence: [^11]

### 2. Obtain any required USDA-endorsed export certificate

Conditional · Within the destination's certificate window; before departure

Species: dogs and cats, subject to the qualifications below.

- The accredited veterinarian completes the destination certificate and submits the required evidence for USDA endorsement, normally through VEHCS.
- Carry the endorsed paper certificate; allow return-shipping time where digital endorsement is not accepted. There is no single validity window for all destinations.

Evidence: [^11]

### 3. Preserve the dog's high-risk return option

Conditional · Before the dog leaves the United States

Species: dog only.

- If high-risk-country travel is planned, have the specific U.S.-issued rabies certification completed and USDA-endorsed before departure.
- Keep vaccination coverage continuous and reassess the six-month travel history for return; the export certificate alone is not the current CDC return document.

Evidence: [^4]

## Caveats

- DRAFT pending owner review; researched guidance is not route approval.
- Cargo carriage alone does not mean a commercial sale or resale movement.
- Federal dog requirements must never be copied onto cats.
- Hawaii and Guam require their own itinerary assessment; no local quarantine exemption is inferred from country code alone.

## Conflicts and remaining verification gaps

- State and municipal rules, breed restrictions, carrier acceptance and every bilateral/transit itinerary have not been assessed.
- CDC registered-facility capacity, APHIS affected-region lists and local permit conditions must be rechecked for the actual date.
- Hawaii's older FAQ contains inconsistent waiting-period statements; use the current landing page and route checklist, and obtain authority confirmation for an exemption or conflicting timing.

These gaps are not waived by the research date. The relevant authority, certificate or issued permit must resolve them before owner approval or individual travel advice.
