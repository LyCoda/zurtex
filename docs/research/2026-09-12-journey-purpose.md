# Pet journeys: purpose, ownership and the right process
Research addendum · 12 September 2026 · Local research, not publication-approved guidance

## Findings

Zurtex should ask people what they are doing, rather than ask them to choose a legal category. Travelling with an already-owned pet, moving home with that pet, acquiring a new pet, transporting animals for sale, and travelling for breeding or an event are understandable descriptions. Whether any of those movements meets a particular government’s personal-pet, commercial-import, customs or business-licensing rules is a separate question.

There is no reliable worldwide mapping from “breeder”, “adoption”, “permanent relocation” or “cargo” to a single legal classification. The evidence below contains direct counterexamples. A guide that makes that mapping automatically will both frighten ordinary pet owners and understate genuinely different obligations.

The existing 11 September dossiers remain the country-by-country record of animal-health entry and departure research. This addendum investigates purpose and presentation; it does not claim to replace those dossiers with fully verified commercial procedures for every ordered route. The selector contains 23 jurisdictions, not 22 countries: each jurisdiction has 22 other destination choices.

## Why the earlier answer felt like a breeder shipment

The local composer already used personal-pet dossiers for holidays and relocation. The problem was largely framing: “export” instructions, large legal caveats, repeated certification tasks and fully expanded paragraphs dominated the result. A traveller had to infer that the same certificate could be necessary for their family pet.

An official export certificate describes movement out of a jurisdiction. Its name alone does not establish a sale or breeding purpose. For example, Singapore’s personal-pet guidance still requires certification endorsed by the exporting government veterinarian, and China has a named non-commercial pet-export service.[^8] The appropriate response is a plain-language explanation and the exact document name together—not removal of a real certificate.

The design critique also identified a task-selection problem. The purpose choice was buried in step two; users did not see a clear distinction between travel, relocation and new-owner/business purposes. This addendum informs the explicit first-step choice and the bounded review branch.

## Country distinctions

### European Union: France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, Portugal and Spain

The Commission links non-commercial movement to the owner’s journey and absence of an intended sale or ownership transfer. An authorised companion can travel within five days of the owner. Different flights or carriage in the hold can qualify when the responsibility conditions are met. Its current FAQ also allows some newly acquired pets returning with their new owner to qualify, so “adoption” is not a universal trade verdict.[^1]

Implementation consequence: keep intent and legal classification separate; require purpose review for a new-owner scenario instead of asserting that every adoption is commercial. Do not apply this shared scope definition as proof that all seven destinations have identical health, breed or entry-point rules.

### Great Britain

GOV.UK directs sale, rehoming and ownership transfer, owner travel more than five days apart, and groups above five without an eligible event exception to extra rules. Basic pet-health conditions still matter. These rules do not classify every household relocation as a sale. Northern Ireland is outside the Great Britain selector.[^2]

The Balai guidance adds the commercial movement process, including relevant registration, documentation and notification. A three-step preparation brief is not a substitute for that full process.[^3]

### Switzerland and Norway

Swiss guidance supports authorised-person travel within five days of the owner, with written documentation, and has a permit route for temporary participation with more than five animals in competitions or similar events. National customs and commercial-import conditions remain separate.[^4]

Norway explicitly permits freight within the owner’s five-day travel window with evidence of the owner’s journey. Failure to meet the private-travel timing or documentation conditions directs the traveller to commercial-import rules. Freight alone is not the trigger.[^5]

### United States and Canada

USDA APHIS’s updated 2 June 2026 guidance concerns the Animal Welfare Act dog-resale regime. It excludes, among other cases, breeding stock not resold, show/event dogs, and foreign-purchased or adopted dogs delivered to final owners who will not resell them. CDC and other federal/state controls remain separate. These are dog-specific findings, not rules for cats.[^6]

Canada’s CFIA instead includes breeding, show/exhibition, adoption, fostering, transfer and resale among commercial dog purposes; high-risk dog-rabies origins face restrictions in that category. A same-owner show dog cannot be assumed to follow the same classification as in the United States. Age, origin and precise end use still need checking.[^7]

### Singapore

AVS separates personal and commercial import licences. For Schedule II, owner timing and shared residence affect the personal-licence quarantine branch; personal animals not meeting those conditions do not automatically become commercial imports. Commercial-licence animals have a different quarantine arrangement. These distinctions sit alongside origin/history and health preparation.[^8]

Implementation consequence: a simple relocation checkbox cannot establish a quarantine exemption. The guide must preserve missing residence history and the distinction between a licence category and a medical preparation sequence.

### Australia and New Zealand

Australian Border Force defines animals imported for breeding, sale, racing or competition as commercial animals for customs treatment. Previously owned pets arriving with unaccompanied personal effects can still have personal treatment. The source explicitly separates biosecurity requirements and Customs clearance.[^9]

New Zealand Customs includes breeding, showing and racing among commercial reasons and directs those importers to a Customs broker. MPI biosecurity requirements remain a separate layer. Neither finding is a reason to replace the animal-health dossier with a generic commercial vaccine schedule.[^10]

## Starting-country obligations

Purpose must be checked at both ends. The departure authority may require certification, endorsement, an export permit or an importer’s destination approval even when a traveller retains ownership. The pre-existing 23 departure dossiers remain available for that work. A destination’s definition must not be applied as the starting country’s business licence or customs classification.

For an ordinary personal journey, the local answer retains destination requirements and relevant departure preparation. One recognised duplicate—GB-issued AHC preparation for an EU journey—is consolidated into the certificate action while retaining both source sets. Return preparation is conditional for a visit and not presented as mandatory when the user selects moving home.

For a breeding/new-owner/event journey, the software withholds the standard personal veterinary sequence as a complete answer. It asks the user to confirm the applicable process, agree paperwork with both authorities, then arrange the approved transport/arrival procedure. Specific unanswered licences, health work and certificate dates remain visibly unresolved.

## Implementation decisions and remaining facts

The visible choices are “Travelling with my pet”, “Moving home with my pet”, and “Breeding or a new owner”. The latter explicitly includes events and reveals a precise purpose selector. Being professionally involved with animals is not itself treated as a legal conclusion.

Pet count and owner timing affect which guide is offered. More than five pets or an owner travelling more than five days apart triggers a conservative review, not a claim that every country uses that cutoff. Event exceptions and countries with smaller per-person limits mean this boundary is intentionally not an eligibility rule.

The current intake does not collect each animal’s age, breed, ownership chronology, residence history, clinical records or a complete multi-leg itinerary. It cannot establish entry permission, quarantine exemption or actual readiness. Adoption before collection, nonprofit breeding and temporary events deserve later structured questions if the product moves toward approved personalised assessments.

The result uses progressive disclosure: actions and timings remain visible, supporting conditions and official sources expand on demand. Important blockers stay outside collapsed content. Reading checkboxes have been removed; they added administration without verifying anything. Flights and sources are separate accessible tabs. The Ready Pack retains its organisational value and full-detail print representation; it is not being launched as a purchased service by this change.

## Research quality and limits

Sources were opened beyond search snippets, prioritising the destination authority and distinguishing health, customs and business licensing. The supplementary eight-jurisdiction note documents its exact official pages, dated documents and source scope. Existing country dossiers preserve earlier conflicts, access failures and research dates rather than being silently overwritten.

The strongest supported conclusion is the need to separate intent from jurisdiction-specific process. There is insufficient evidence to certify a complete commercial or breeding route for all 506 ordered pairs. The BAI Charter is older than this research date and needs current implementation confirmation. Customs pages may retain older agency names; their classification evidence is not proof that every linked procedural detail is current.
